# Meme catalog

Pick formats by the **question a scene answers**, not by which meme is
funniest. Each format's schema (fields, limits, field descriptions) follows
this catalog. **Joke** formats are for `light` stories only; **straight**
formats fit any story, and are the only ones a `serious` story may use.

| Reader question | Template | Needs from the article | Skip when |
|---|---|---|---|
| What's changing? | `distracted` | someone turning from one thing to another | the article reports no shift, only your reading of one |
| What did they choose? | `nope-yep` | a choice someone in the story made, with what they turned down | you'd be choosing for them |
| What's the dilemma? | `two-buttons` | someone facing two options the article names | the article gives only one option |
| Did the numbers move? | `stonks` | 1–3 figures that rose or fell, as the article says | the article gives no direction, or the numbers count people harmed |
| How big is it getting? | `expanding-brain` | 3–4 steps, each bigger than the last | the steps aren't growing, or it would rank people or opinions |
| What are the essentials? | `starter-pack` | 3+ short, concrete facts about one thing | the facts are about people's hardship |
| What's their stance? | `change-my-mind` | a direct quote stating a firm position, from a named speaker | the quote is filler, or the speaker is a private person in distress |
| What do we know so far? | `megathread` | 2+ short facts, and optionally dated updates | the facts would only restate the headline |
| Who said what? | `thread` | 1–4 direct quotes from named speakers | the quotes are filler ("We are pleased to announce…") |
| What happens next? | `remindme` | 1+ dates ahead, as the article gives them | nothing is scheduled or expected |

If no row fits a question, leave the question out. Fewer, sharper memes beat
a full page.

## Joke formats

**`distracted`**: the distracted-boyfriend meme. `who` turns away from `old`
toward `new`. Use it for a shift the article reports: a company changing
course, travellers switching modes, a council dropping one plan for another.
Labels are fragments of a few words.

**`nope-yep`**: the "no, yes" two-panel meme. `who` turns down `nope` and
picks `yep`. The choice must be one someone in the story made or reported,
attributed in `who` ("The council", "Shoppers, says the chain"). Never your
own preference.

**`two-buttons`**: the sweating two-buttons meme. `who` faces `left` or
`right`. Both options must come from the article; give them equal weight.

**`stonks`**: one to three trading screens, each a number with an arrow. Use
it for money, markets, prices, sales and counts of things. The arrow shows
direction only, never good or bad: losses falling is a down arrow. Never use
it for counts of people harmed; those stories are serious anyway.

**`expanding-brain`**: three or four steps, each bigger in size, scale or
reach: a rollout from 12 stores to 120, passenger numbers year by year. Put
the year or date in the text. Never use it to rank opinions or people from
dumb to smart.

**`starter-pack`**: the essentials of one thing, as a collage: "The night
train starter pack". Each piece is a concrete fact with an emoji. Not for
stories about people's hardship.

**`change-my-mind`**: one person's stance, word for word, on a sign at their
table. Only for a firm position from someone speaking in public: an
executive, an official, an analyst. The quote must match the article
exactly.

## Straight formats

**`megathread`**: the pinned megathread big subreddits run for breaking
news. `facts` are label and value, like "Homes flooded: About 200";
`updates` are dated developments, newest first. The workhorse for serious
stories, and fine for light ones too.

**`thread`**: quotes as a comment thread. Each comment is a direct quote,
with the speaker as the article names them and their role as a flair. Set
`reply` only when the article presents a quote as a response to, or
pushback on, the one above. Lead with the main voice.

**`remindme`**: the dates ahead, soonest first, as the article writes them
("Wednesday", "by March", "later this year").

## Ordering

1. Lead with the scene that answers the main question.
2. Then the evidence: `stonks`, `expanding-brain`, `megathread`.
3. Then reaction and what's next: `thread`, `change-my-mind`, `remindme`.

## Pairings that often work

These are starting points, not rules.

| Story | Scenes |
|---|---|
| Company changes course | `distracted` + `stonks` + `thread` |
| Policy decision | `nope-yep` + `two-buttons` + `remindme` |
| Market or economic data | `stonks` + `expanding-brain` |
| New product or service | `starter-pack` + `stonks` + `change-my-mind` |
| Growth story | `expanding-brain` + `starter-pack` + `thread` |
| Disaster, accident or crime (serious) | `megathread` + `remindme` + `thread` |
| Health or conflict news (serious) | `megathread` + `thread` |
