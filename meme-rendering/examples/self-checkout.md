# Supermarket chain swaps self-checkouts for staffed tills in 120 stores

*This is a fictional article written to test it's news. The company, the
people and the figures are invented.*

Pantry & Co, a supermarket chain with 310 stores, will replace most of its
self-checkout machines with staffed tills in 120 stores by March, the company
said on Wednesday, after a six-month trial in 12 stores.

In the trial stores, losses from theft and scanning mistakes fell 31%, and
the average wait to pay dropped from 6 minutes to 4 minutes. Sales of fresh
food rose 5%. The chain will hire about 900 cashiers.

"People told us they were tired of arguing with a machine about an
unexpected item in the bagging area," said Marta Lindqvist, Pantry & Co's
chief executive. "Our customers would rather talk to a person."

The chain installed its first self-checkouts in 2016 to cut staff costs. By
2024, the machines handled 6 in 10 of its sales.

The company estimates that the staffed tills will cost about £40 million a
year, against savings of about £52 million a year from lower losses.

The first 40 stores switch in November and the rest by March. Every store
will keep at least two self-checkout machines for shoppers buying a few
items.

Not everyone is sure the switch will pay off. Priya Nair, a retail analyst at
Example Retail Research, said staffed tills "cost more every single hour,
and the savings from lower theft may not last". She expects other chains to
wait for a full year of results before they follow.
