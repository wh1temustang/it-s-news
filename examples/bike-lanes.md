# Port Ellery votes to keep its downtown bike lanes after pilot

*This is a fictional article written to test it's news. Port Ellery, its
council and the figures are invented.*

Port Ellery's city council voted 7-4 on Tuesday to make the downtown
protected bike lanes permanent, ending an 18-month pilot that divided
businesses and residents.

The lanes, 6.5 km of separated track along Harbour Street and Main Avenue,
were installed in November 2024 for $2.3 million. Making them permanent, with
concrete curbs in place of the plastic posts, will cost another $4.1 million.

Cycling trips on the two streets averaged 1,200 a day in the month before the
pilot. City counters recorded 2,100 a day in spring 2025, 2,800 in autumn
2025 and 3,400 in spring 2026.

The city's report found that collisions causing injury on the corridor fell
from 31 in the 18 months before the pilot to 17 during it. Average rush-hour
driving time along Main Avenue rose from 9 minutes to 11 minutes.

Retail results were mixed. Card spending at shops on the corridor rose 6%
compared with the same period before the pilot, while restaurants on the
corridor reported a 3% drop. Across the city, card spending rose 4%.

"The numbers made the decision for us," said Mayor Dana Whitcombe. "Fewer
people are getting hurt, and more people are coming downtown."

Councillor Ray Abbot, who voted against, said the lanes had "taken parking
away from the people who keep Main Avenue alive" and asked city staff to
report on delivery loading zones by September.

Work on the permanent curbs is scheduled to begin in March 2027 and finish by
the end of 2028.
